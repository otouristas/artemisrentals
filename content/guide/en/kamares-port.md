Kamares is where Sifnos begins. Every ferry docks here, so every visitor sets foot on the island at this one quay. What surprises most of them is that Kamares is not merely a transit point: it is a working port wrapped around a long sandy beach, with tavernas, bakeries and rooms, and it makes a perfectly good place to stay in its own right.

![Kamares beach and port](/images/sifnos/beaches/kamares.webp "Kamares, the port village and its sandy bay")

## Where Kamares sits

The village occupies a deep, sheltered bay on the **west coast** of Sifnos, hemmed in by hills on both sides. That shelter is why the port is here, and it is also why the swimming is so calm.

The layout is simple. The ferry quay is at one end, the beach stretches away from it along the bay, and a single waterfront road runs between the two lined with almost everything you need. You cannot get lost.

**Apollonia**, the island capital, is about 5.5 km inland and uphill, roughly ten minutes by car on a well-surfaced but winding road.

## First steps off the ferry

1. Clear the ramp and move away from the vehicle deck, which unloads quickly and with little ceremony.
2. Meet your car if you have arranged **Kamares pickup**, or head for the bus and taxi area just beyond the quay.
3. If your accommodation check-in is later, drop your bags and swim. The beach is a two minute walk.
4. Note the ticket offices along the waterfront for your return sailing.

Arrivals can feel chaotic for a few minutes when a large ferry unloads in August, with cars, scooters, luggage and hotel transfer boards all in the same small space. It settles within a quarter of an hour.

## The beach

Kamares beach is long, sandy and shallow, entering the water gently for a considerable distance. That combination makes it **one of the two best beaches on Sifnos for young children**, alongside Platys Gialos, and it is the only good beach you can reach on foot from a ferry.

There are seasonal sunbeds and umbrellas at the central section, with quieter unorganised sand towards the far end. Tamarisk trees provide patchy natural shade along the back.

Because the bay faces west and is enclosed by high ground, it stays notably calm when the **meltemi** is blowing from the north. On a windy day it is a reliable fallback, second only to Vathi.

## What is at the port

- **Tavernas and cafés** along the entire waterfront, several open from early morning
- A **bakery** and a couple of **mini-markets** for supplies
- **Rooms and small hotels**, many within sight of the quay
- **Ferry ticket offices** and, in season, port information
- **Pottery workshops**, continuing the tradition Kamares has held for generations
- **Trailheads** for the walking route up towards Agios Symeon

There is no bank branch, though ATMs are available. For a wider range of shops and restaurants you want Apollonia.

## Should you stay in Kamares?

It is a genuinely good choice in three situations: your ferry arrives late at night, your ferry leaves early in the morning, or you are travelling with small children who want sand without a drive.

The trade-off is that the island's best restaurants and its only real evening scene are up in Apollonia and Artemonas, fifteen minutes away by car. Kamares is relaxed rather than lively after dark.

## Getting up to Apollonia and beyond

This is the practical problem every arriving visitor faces, and it is worth understanding before you land.

**The bus** meets most ferries and runs up to Apollonia, where the island's routes converge. It works, but it follows its own timetable rather than the boat's, and it fills quickly when a full ferry lands.

**Taxis** exist, but Sifnos has only a handful of them for the entire island. When a large August ferry unloads, they are gone within minutes and there is no queue system worth the name.

**Your own vehicle** removes the problem entirely.

## Parking in Kamares

There is parking along the waterfront and in informal areas behind the beach. It is tight in the middle of the day in August, and tightest in the half hour either side of a ferry arrival or departure. Do not block the quay access road, it stays busy with freight.
