Sifnos is small, roughly 14 km end to end, but where you sleep changes the shape of your holiday more than you would expect. The island splits into a central inland cluster of villages up on the ridge and a scatter of coastal settlements below, and the two experiences are genuinely different. This guide compares every realistic base.

![Apollonia](/images/sifnos/villages/apollonia.webp "Apollonia, the island capital and the busiest evening scene on Sifnos")

## Apollonia: the default choice, and usually the right one

Apollonia sits high on the island's central ridge and is where Sifnos actually lives. The pedestrianised main lane, **Stenaki**, is lined with restaurants, bars, bakeries and shops, and it is the one place on the island with genuine evening buzz that runs late in July and August.

Being central is the practical argument. From Apollonia nowhere on Sifnos is more than about 25 minutes away, so you can beach-hop in the day and walk to dinner at night without moving the car again. It is also the island's transport hub, where the bus routes converge.

The trade-offs: no beach, some accommodation sits on stepped lanes that cars cannot reach, and rooms on or near Stenaki can be noisy in peak season. Ask about the walk from the nearest parking before you book, especially if you have heavy luggage.

Our office is in Apollonia, so if you stay here your keys are a short walk away.

## Artemonas: Apollonia's quieter neighbour

![Artemonas](/images/sifnos/villages/artemonas.webp "Artemonas, neoclassical houses and quiet lanes")

Artemonas is a fifteen minute walk north of Apollonia and feels a world apart. It is the handsome one: neoclassical mansions, bougainvillea, wide flagstoned lanes and a slower pace. There are several excellent tavernas and a couple of the island's best patisseries.

It suits people who want to be near the action without sleeping in it. You can walk into Apollonia for dinner and walk home. Free car delivery is included here.

## Kamares: the port, and better than that sounds

Arriving travellers sometimes dismiss Kamares as merely a ferry terminal. It is not. The bay holds a long sandy beach with shallow, sheltered water that is genuinely one of the best family swimming spots on the island, and the waterfront is lined with tavernas, a bakery and a good supermarket.

Choose Kamares if you have a late-arriving or early-departing ferry, if you are travelling with small children who want sand without a drive, or if you simply like waking up at sea level. The one drawback is that it is a fifteen minute drive from the island's better restaurants up in Apollonia and Artemonas.

## Platys Gialos: the beach holiday

![Platys Gialos](/images/sifnos/beaches/plats-gialos.webp "Platys Gialos, the longest beach on Sifnos")

The longest beach on Sifnos, about 20 minutes south of Apollonia, with a line of hotels, rooms, tavernas and beach bars set directly behind the sand. If your ideal holiday is walking from your room to a sunbed and not thinking about it again, this is your base.

It is a resort in the low-key Cycladic sense, not a party strip. Families and couples both do well here. You will still want wheels to reach Kastro, the north coast and the villages, since Platys Gialos itself is a single road with one thing on it.

## Vathi: the calm one

![Vathi](/images/sifnos/beaches/vathi.webp "Vathi, a sheltered sandy bay on the southwest coast")

A deep, sheltered horseshoe bay on the southwest coast with fine sand and unusually calm water, protected even when the meltemi is blowing elsewhere. Vathi was reachable only by boat or mule track until relatively recently, and it retains that end-of-the-road quiet.

Perfect for couples and anyone who wants to read a book undisturbed. Correspondingly, there is very little to do after dinner and you will be driving for any variety at all. Pickup here is on request rather than free, and the fee is confirmed when you book.

## Faros and Chrysopigi: small and swimmable

Faros is a modest fishing settlement with three small beaches within a few minutes' walk of each other, a few tavernas and a genuinely local feel. It is also the walking access point to **Chrysopigi**, the clifftop monastery that appears on every postcard of Sifnos, roughly fifteen minutes from Apollonia by car.

Good for travellers who want to swim daily, eat simply and skip the scene entirely.

## Kastro: the view, at a price

![Kastro](/images/sifnos/villages/kastro.webp "Kastro, the medieval capital on its clifftop")

The medieval capital, a walled village wrapped around a headland with sea on three sides, Venetian coats of arms above doorways and views that stop conversations. There are a small number of rooms inside the village.

Understand what you are choosing: no vehicle access inside the walls, luggage carried on foot, no beach beyond rocky swimming below, and almost nothing open late. In exchange you get to be there after the day visitors leave, which is the entire point. Free car delivery reaches Kastro.

## So which should you pick?

**First time on Sifnos, one week, want to see it all:** Apollonia or Artemonas.

**Travelling with young children:** Kamares or Platys Gialos, for sand without a drive.

**Couples wanting quiet:** Vathi, Kastro or Faros.

**Two or three nights only:** Apollonia, because you will waste less time moving.

**Arriving on a late ferry:** Kamares for the first night at least.

## Do you need a car?

From Platys Gialos, Vathi, Faros or Kastro, effectively yes. Buses run but they are infrequent, they fill in August, and they will dictate your day. From Apollonia and Artemonas you could manage without one for a short stay, though you would see a fraction of the island.

The honest answer is that Sifnos rewards a car more than most Cycladic islands, because the good things are spread out and the distances are short enough that having wheels turns a 40 minute bus wait into a 12 minute drive.
