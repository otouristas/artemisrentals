Sifnos is small enough to cross in under an hour and interesting enough that you will want to stop every twenty minutes. Driving here is not difficult, but it is not mainland motorway driving either. Narrow asphalt, occasional gravel stretches, and goats that treat the verge as a footpath all belong to the experience.

## Road character across the island

Sifnos roads do not form a coastal loop. They radiate from **Apollonia** on the central ridge like spokes: west down to Kamares, south to Platys Gialos, southeast to Faros and Chrysopigi, southwest to Vathi, east to Kastro, and north through Artemonas to Cherronisos. Most days you drive out to a beach and back up to the centre, which keeps navigation simple.

The main spokes are sealed and in good condition, but they are narrow, winding and often without barriers. Leaving the coast means a steady climb back to the ridge, so manual drivers should be comfortable with hill starts and second gear, and automatic renters will appreciate not juggling the clutch on the bends above Kamares. The roads to Vathi and Cherronisos are the longest and twistiest; the last stretches to a few coves, such as Vroulidia, can be rough.

Village centres like Apollonia and Artemonas have narrow streets and tight corners. Park at the edges and walk in. Do not attempt to thread a car through the lanes of Kastro: they are pedestrian, so park outside the village and walk.

### Night driving

First evening after a ferry arrival, keep driving minimal. Street lighting is sparse outside Kamares and Apollonia, goats wander onto the road after dark, and unmarked junctions appear where farm tracks meet the main road. We recommend settling in, then starting full island loops the next morning.

## Parking at beaches and villages

Peak August at Platys Gialos, Vathi, and Chrysopigi rewards early arrivals. Aim for before 10 a.m. on hot weeks, or after 4 p.m. when the first wave of swimmers leaves. Faros and Cherronisos have smaller parking areas that fill quickly on summer evenings when people drive in for fish tavernas.

### Parking village by village

| Place | What to expect | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Apollonia** | Narrow centre, pedestrian main lane, busy from 8 p.m. in summer | Park at the edge of the village once for the evening and walk. Ask your hotel where its guests park. |
| **Artemonas** | Quieter, but streets are narrow and residential | Use the parking at the village edge; do not block courtyard gates. |
| **Kastro** | Car-free medieval lanes inside the walls | Park outside the village and walk in. Arrive early for sunset in August. |
| **Kamares** | Waterfront and informal parking behind the beach | Tightest around ferry arrivals and departures. Never block the quay access road. |
| **Platys Gialos** | The island's busiest beach | Before 10 a.m. or after 4 p.m.; otherwise park on a side street and walk. |
| **Vathi** | Village parking near the bay | Arrive before lunch in August. |
| **Chrysopigi** | Limited spaces near the monastery | Never block the chapel access; combine with Faros. |
| **Faros, Cherronisos** | Small lots, tight lanes | Reverse carefully and leave room for delivery vans. |

## Fuel, returns, and rental logistics

Petrol stations are few, so fill up before a long day out if your gauge is below half, and ask us at pickup where the nearest pump is. Return the car with the same fuel level as at pickup unless we agreed otherwise. **No prepayment** is required when you enquire, and car pickup and return are free at **Kamares port**, the heliport, **Apollonia**, Artemonas and Kastro. Many guests collect at the port as they step off the ferry and drop the car there before sailing home.

If you are thinking about a scooter instead of a car, read our [licence rules](/en/blog/scooter-licence-rules-sifnos) first: a car licence does not cover a 125cc scooter in Greece. Hills with a passenger on a 125cc are manageable but slower than they look on a map.
